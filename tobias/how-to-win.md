# How to win

Tobias's working note on Elon Musk's "how to win" principles: the principles, his reflection, and open threads. Personal and meant to grow; add dated entries under Log rather than rewriting the reflection.

## Core

Risk-taking and hard work are the two that land hardest for Tobias (2026-10-18). Without them he sees himself going nowhere. Everything else in this note supports these two.

> There's a tremendous bias against taking risks. Everyone is trying to optimize their ass-covering.

> Work like hell. You just have to put in 80 to 100 hour weeks every week. This improves the odds of success.

> When something is important enough, you do it even if the odds are not in your favor.

## The principles

**Taking risk**
- If things are not failing, you are not innovating enough.
- There's a tremendous bias against taking risks. Everyone is trying to optimize their ass-covering.
- Embrace change if the alternative is disaster.
- First establish that something is possible; then probability will occur.

**Hard work**
- Don't give up unless you are forced to.
- Work like hell: 80 to 100 hour weeks improve the odds of success.
- When something is important enough, do it even if the odds are against you.

**Leadership**
- The path to the CEO's office should run through engineering and design, not the CFO's office or marketing.
- People work better when they know the goal and why, and when they look forward to coming to work.
- As a founder or CEO, no task is too menial.

**Personal growth**
- Keep a feedback loop: constantly think about what you've done and how you could do it better.
- Pay attention to negative feedback and solicit it, particularly from friends.
- Ordinary people can choose to be extraordinary.
- If you get up in the morning and think the future is going to be better, it is a bright day.
- Pursue what you're passionate about.

**Entrepreneurship**
- A business is as much the innovation, drive and determination of its people as the product.
- Being an entrepreneur is like eating glass and staring into the abyss of death.
- A breakthrough is rarely one little thing; it is many things that together amount to a huge innovation.
- Talent wins, multiplied by how players work together and the strategy they employ.
- Hiring huge numbers of people for a complicated job is a mistake. Numbers will never compensate for talent in getting the right answer.

## Reflection (2026-10-18)

### Already living it

- **Numbers never compensate for talent.** The same bet as one frontier-model agent doing the whole task itself, only coordinators and large-project leads dividing work into whole outcomes, and refusing to split one design across many workers. Talent is the strongest model plus full context; the multiplier is orchestration and strategy.
- **Anti ass-covering.** No shims, fallbacks or legacy paths unless a contract demands them; clean breaks over defensive code. That is refusing to optimize ass-covering, applied to codebases.
- **Feedback loop.** Autoresearch, retros and agent-history reviews are this principle turned into machinery. The vault itself is the loop's memory.
- **Path through engineering and design.** Tobias decides through the product and the system, not through spreadsheets or pitch decks. Keep it that way as anything grows.
- **Possible first, then probable.** A prototype answers one question: can this work at all? Hardening comes after.

### Needs translating for the agent era

- **80–100 hour weeks.** Keep the hours and multiply them. Tobias's own hours go to what agents can't supply: intent, decisions, challenge, taste. Agents run around the clock beside him, so "work like hell" means both: put in the hours, and keep decision latency low enough that the fleet never idles waiting for him. Count shipped loops and decisions, not just time at the desk.
- **Direction before effort.** Hours and risk compound only when aimed at the right thing. Working like hell on the wrong bet still goes nowhere, so recheck each week that the hardest work goes to the biggest bet.
- **No task too menial.** Delegating everything risks losing contact with the ground truth. Doing the menial thing yourself sometimes, such as using the product as a user, reading raw transcripts, or onboarding a customer by hand, shows what no report surfaces.
- **Many small things make a breakthrough.** Compounding is the strategy: every skill, check, vault note and tool makes the next run better. Don't wait for one big idea.

### Gaps worth pushing on

- **Is enough failing?** Most of the system guards against failure: gates, no new failures versus main, landing agents. That is right for production. The test is whether the experimental side has a visible kill rate. If no bet failed this month, the bets were too safe.
- **Negative feedback from friends.** Agents are tuned to be blunt, but they have no stake and no taste of their own. Solicit criticism from people who do: users, peers, friends who will say the product is bad.
- **Eating glass.** The quote describes a cost, not a virtue. The aim is to survive the abyss long enough for compounding to work, not to romanticize it.
