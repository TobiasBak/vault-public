# Unslop

Make writing concise, clear, and natural. Preserve the point and the author's voice, not every word or qualification.

## Edit passages before sentences

- Lead with the answer, claim, or action. Cut introductions that only announce what follows.
- Remove repeated ideas, empty framing, recaps, and explanations that add no new fact, distinction, reason, example, or instruction.
- Keep conditions and caveats that matter to the reader. Fold them into the claim; cut repetitive qualifications and separate disclaimer paragraphs.
- Preserve the meaning and useful detail. Do not invent stronger claims or turn suggestions into requirements. Ordinary writing does not need research-report precision.
- Keep examples and rationale that explain a difficult distinction. Do not shorten a useful passage into a vague summary or chase a word quota.
- Shorten the explanation itself. Splitting a bloated sentence leaves bloated prose. Group related thoughts and remove clauses the reader can infer without guessing.
- Write whole sentences with articles and verbs. Do not make readers decode fragments, arrows, or unexplained abbreviations for the sake of brevity.
- Keep vivid wording that carries meaning. Do not casually rephrase quotations, code, commands, identifiers, or precise domain terms.

## Plain, natural language

- Name the actor, action, mechanism, or result. Prefer active voice unless the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Use the shortest ordinary word that fits. Write "uses," not "leverages"; "is," not "serves as." Keep technical terms when a simpler word would change the meaning.
- Cut puffery, promotional language, stale metaphors, abstract nouns, and vague praise. Replace claims such as "significantly faster" with the measured change when available; do not invent precision.
- Prefer literal descriptions to personified code, decorative fragments, or clever slogans that obscure what happens.
- Replace vague attribution with the relevant source. State real evidence gaps directly instead of hiding them behind boilerplate.
- Remove redundant hedges, adverbs, formulaic contrasts, false ranges, and synonym cycling. Do not force ideas into groups of three.
- Match the voice to the artifact. Conversation can be blunt, opinionated, or funny; documentation and external communication should suit their readers. Do not add personality, opinions, or deliberate mess just to seem human.
- Cut canned greetings, flattery, reassurance, and generic conclusions. Vary rhythm without adding filler.

## Style

- Avoid em dashes, en dashes, parentheses, and hyphens used as dashes. Use periods or commas to separate thoughts.
- Use colons before lists or examples, not as unnecessary sentence connectors.
- Use sentence-case headings and straight quotes. Avoid decorative emojis and excessive bold.
- Use lists and tables when they make relationships clearer. Remove labels that merely repeat the sentence they introduce.

## Final read

Read it as a reader. Is it clear, natural, and concise without losing the point? Stop when it works. Return the edited text without an editing report unless requested.
